# Source coverage and editorial decisions

This is one skill with independent entry points. The sources below correspond to the editions reviewed for Folio. The text-setting benchmark is Matthew Butterick’s *Practical Typography*. The agent skill interpreting it is by **danium**. Presets and checks contributed by skill authors are identified separately from Butterick’s advice and from accessibility criteria. [Butterick’s guide](https://practicaltypography.com/) · [Butterick on body text](https://practicaltypography.com/body-text.html)

## Where the sources disagree

| Decision | Guidance in tension | Folio’s choice |
|---|---|---|
| What to set first | Practical Typography begins with the body. Editorial Grid begins with page geometry. | If the sheet or binding is fixed, establish it first and fit a real reading paragraph. If the format is open, use the paragraph to propose the sheet. Iterate until text and page work together. |
| Line length, size and leading | Practical Typography gives 45–90 characters, 10–12 pt print and 120–145% leading. Editorial Grid favors 45–75 characters for a single column, about 66, with leading up to 150% on long lines. Guillot allows smaller text in a book or magazine and often more leading on screen. | Start moderate, measure full lines within one role, and judge actual return sweeps. Smaller magazine text needs suitable x-height, stock and distance. A 15 px compact screen and a long-reading face at 20 px deserve separate proofs. |
| Margins, columns and gutters | Editorial Grid favors named, asymmetric canons and suggests about 2 em for gutters. Guillot also discusses gutters near one em or one baseline. Practical Typography works outward from content. | Try the canon on a bound spread. Test both gutter widths on real neighboring columns. In a brief, table, slide or phone screen, preserve alignment, distinct columns and useful whitespace. The author owns the choice of symmetry. |
| Baselines, scale and captions | Editorial Grid asks that every level and element follow a named scale and rhythm. Guillot supplies precise numerical tests. Practical Typography leaves many supporting roles to visual calibration. | Use strict grids for spreads that benefit from aligned lines. Use a scale to propose roles. Confirm every caption, table note and heading independently. A fixed step down may make the only explanatory note illegible. Retain optical adjustments that improve the visible edge. |
| Emphasis, ligatures, links and numbers | Prescriptive microtypography can call for ligatures, oldstyle figures and underlined links throughout. Practical Typography conditions substitutions and features on the content. | Inspect ligatures for collisions in prose, preserve exact literals, use tabular figures for columns, and keep a reliable link cue in prose. Language, meaning and accessibility govern changes to glyphs. |
| Tone and alignment | Press advice calls for black text and often favors justification. Screen advice often calls for softened colors and a ragged reading edge. | Choose for the destination. Test single-plate text on press, contrast on screen, and justification with the actual language, hyphenation and measure. |
| What stays together | The print checklist keeps tables and figures intact. A long data table can exceed a whole page. | Keep a short table with its caption or note. For a long table, break between rows, repeat headers and units, and make continuation unambiguous. |
| How to score and report | Practical Typography ranks findings by their actual impact. Guillot has four severity bands and limits the report to the most important findings. | Lead with up to three consequential corrections. Include every blocker to meaning, access or production. Keep minor polish secondary. Report the evidence and the smallest specific fix. |

Use the reader’s language, the meaning of the text, verifiable accessibility criteria, font licensing and the actual delivery constraints to resolve hard conflicts. Within those constraints the author chooses the face, rhythm, density and degree of formality. Show a side-by-side specimen when both choices work.
